# Future moderated pilot protocol — not yet conducted

Plan for approximately 6–8 voluntarily consenting learners and 35–50 minutes each. The purpose is qualitative usability, comprehension, workflow and immediate content perception. There is no recruitment/contact/calendar tooling. Seek voluntary variation in typing experience, keyboard familiarity, English familiarity and desktop typing frequency, without demographic assumptions or sensitive attributes. If Pa’O/Myanmar learners participate, language/content behavior is exploratory: the adaptive bank is English-oriented, and neutral Unicode drills are not fluent Pa’O lessons.

## Consent script

“Thank you for considering this optional product usability test. We want to see how the app’s recommendations and typing flow make sense to you. We will observe typing performance and how you use controls; recommendations can be based on typing mistakes. This is not an academic or medical assessment. You can pause, skip a task or stop at any time, without explanation. We use observations to improve product design. Use the supplied practice text; please do not share private text, names, account information or credentials. Data uses a study pseudonym, stays local and is exported only by the facilitator. Nothing is secretly recorded. May we proceed?”

Ask separately: “May I write brief qualitative notes and direct quotes about the interface, excluding identifying details and typing passages?” Separately ask permission for each desired audio/video/screen recording; participation consent is not recording consent. The tooling records no media. If recording is later used, agree its access, storage, withdrawal and deletion terms before starting. Agree a short data retention period and who can access local exports. Record refusals without pressure. No free notes/quotes if permission is absent; participants can decline any prompt.

## Method and predefined tasks

Use P01–P99, an isolated study workspace and synthetic starting profiles. Tell the learner that seeded prior sessions are not their own performance. Keep facilitator controls out of the participant view. Do not explain a control’s answer before observing its use. Counterbalance T03/T04 where feasible and record task order/help explicitly. Criteria below are fixed before any participant data; TASKS in the tool uses the same criteria. Record assisted outcomes separately from unassisted success.

1. **T01 Ordinary Practice:** “Start a normal typing practice.” Success: find ordinary Practice and begin typing without help. Observe launch/input focus and start-state understanding.
2. **T02 Recommended Practice:** “Choose what the app recommends you do next.” Use active fixture. Success: state the general reason and launch without explanation. Observe Session focus and recommendation comprehension.
3. **T03 Skip:** “Suppose you don’t want to do this exercise right now.” Use active offer. Success: decline and understand progress was not penalized. Do not name Skip until stuck; record any intervention.
4. **T04 Cancel:** Start an exercise, then “Now stop this exercise before finishing it.” Success: stop and distinguish Cancel from Skip without believing it creates learning failure.
5. **T05 Adaptive progression:** “Try the next practice and describe how it differs.” Facilitator explicitly loads active → improving → mixed fixtures in the same task. Success: recognize concentrated/contextual/mixed differences; subjective quality is recorded, not treated as efficacy. Ask whether visible Level language helps or confuses. Fixture changes are synthetic, not measured improvement.
6. **T06 Mastery Check:** Load assessment-eligible fixture. “Continue with the app recommendation.” Success: explain checking recent improvement, not a permanent final exam, and that declining is allowed. Ask what seems fair/unfair and record volunteered concerns without inferring anxiety from typing.
7. **T07 Optional Secondary:** Complete a short primary, then ask the learner to explain Continue/Finish before choosing. Success: identify supporting activity as optional and Finish as ending here.
8. **T08 Change Mind:** Decline one activity, return later, stop another partway through. Success: recover and find the next explicit action. Observe whether the flow feels helpful or controlling.
9. **T09 Timed Test:** “Run a short timed typing test.” Prefer the existing 15-second option. Success: find Test and explain the distinction from Practice; observe completion/Save/navigation.
10. **T10 Result Interpretation:** “What do you think the app wants you to practice next, and why?” Success: explain the general recommendation reason without coaching. Record explanation accuracy manually.

Record task outcome (success / success-with-help / failed / abandoned), wrong clicks, help, explanation accuracy, confusion tags, milestone times and permitted short qualitative notes. Abandoned usability task is not automatically a failed learning activity. Time is descriptive context, not a cohort performance ranking. For task counts use one final observation per participant/task (latest sequence); preserve earlier attempts as a separate descriptive trail, never as extra participants or independent successes. Mark missing tasks explicitly. With six or eight learners report counts and themes, never statistical significance, causal learning effectiveness or misleading averages.

## Neutral think-aloud prompts

“What are you thinking?” “What do you expect this button to do?” “What does this message mean to you?” “What would you do next?” Wait before intervening. Avoid “Don’t you think this is clear?” “Wouldn’t you click Skip?” and leading explanations. If help is necessary, note the prompt and code success-with-help instead of changing the criterion.

## Content review

Ask about repetition, awkward sentences/fragments, progression differences, check content, obvious target repetition, length and perceived difficulty. Record observed/volunteered themes; do not change generator policy from one comment. Ask whether recommendations/explanations feel useful or repetitive, two activities feel acceptable, normal Practice remains a desired choice, and changing one’s mind feels recoverable. Do not confuse content preference with measured transfer or mastery.

## Post-session interview

1. What did you think the app was trying to help you improve?
2. Did any recommendation feel confusing?
3. What did Mastery Check mean to you?
4. What did you think Skip would do?
5. What did you think Cancel would do?
6. Did exercises feel repetitive?
7. Did later exercises feel different from earlier ones?
8. Did you ever feel unsure what to do next?
9. Was anything distracting?
10. What would you change first?

Use [REPORT_TEMPLATE.md](REPORT_TEMPLATE.md) for descriptive findings. Rank usability issues independently of learning-policy severity: Critical blocks a task or creates an incorrect learning consequence; High causes frequent confusion/significant wrong action; Medium is recoverable confusion; Low is minor wording/polish. Tie proposed UX fixes to observed evidence and explicitly list changes not justified by it. Do not recalibrate learning thresholds from this sample.

## Later efficacy study requirements — not implemented

A separate longitudinal design would need predefined baseline typing measurements, repeated use across sessions/days, natural transfer measurements, explicit outcome definitions, appropriate comparison/design and a sample sufficient for its inference. Use consented pseudonymous aggregate evidence, supplied/public text and no private passage replay. Predefine practice dose, follow-up/attrition handling and outcome analysis before recruitment. This qualitative pilot cannot establish “adaptive learning works.” Policy calibration and scheduling require separately approved evidence and design.
